Property in Greece for foreigners: how to buy a flat or a house by the sea

AFM tax number, bank account, document and engineer checks, notary and cadastre — I walk through buying in Greece step by step and add up all the costs.

Venera Khassanovareal estate consultant · Greece and Bulgaria
GreeceVENI Property in Greece for foreigners: how to buy a flat or a house by the sea
In short
  • Step one is a Greek AFM tax number and a bank account; without them there is no deal.
  • Budget 7–10% on top of the price: 3.09% tax, notary, lawyer, cadastre and agency fee.
  • An engineer's check is a must: unauthorised extensions are the most common problem in Greece.

I bought a house in Greece myself, so I know this path from experience, not from a textbook. Greece asks for more paperwork than Bulgaria, but it all goes smoothly if you take it in order.

Step 1. AFM tax number

Without a Greek tax number (AFM) you cannot open an account, sign a contract or pay the tax. You get it at the tax office or through a lawyer with power of attorney — even without travelling.

Step 2. Bank account and power of attorney

The money for the property goes through a bank, so you need an account with a Greek bank. Many buyers give a lawyer power of attorney, so the lawyer can file documents and even sign the deal on their behalf.

Step 3. Checking the property

This is the most important stage:

  • The lawyer checks title, mortgages, seizures and disputes in the land registry/cadastre.
  • The engineer compares the property with the plans and certifies that there are no unauthorised structures, or that they have been legalised. An “extra” room or an enclosed balcony is common in Greece, and the deal will not be signed without this certificate.
  • An energy performance certificate (PEA) is required.

Step 4. Preliminary contract and deposit

If the checks take time, the parties sign a preliminary contract with a deposit. This protects you from the property being sold to someone else.

Step 5. Tax and signing at the notary

The transfer tax is 3.09% of the higher of two figures: the contract price or the “objective” (tax) value. It is paid before signing. Then the purchase contract is signed at the notary and registered with the cadastre — from that moment you are the owner.

What it all costs

  • transfer tax — 3.09%;
  • notary — around 1% plus VAT;
  • lawyer — 1–1.5% plus VAT;
  • cadastre/registration — around 0.5%;
  • agency — usually 2% plus VAT.

In total, 7–10% on top of the price. New builds benefit from a relief: the 24% VAT is suspended until the end of 2026, and the same 3.09% tax is paid instead.

What to know about 2027

A bill is before parliament: for home buyers who are tax residents outside the EU, the transfer tax would rise from 3% to 15% on deals from 1 July 2027. It is not law yet (more on the new tax), but if you are from outside the EU, plan your purchase early.

Golden Visa

If you buy property worth €250,000 (in specific cases, such as converting a commercial property into a home), you can get a 5-year residence permit for the whole family. For other areas and property types the thresholds are higher — €400,000 and €800,000.
