Bulgaria or Greece: where is it better to buy property in 2027?

I compare the two countries I work in every day: price per square metre, buying costs, rental tax, running costs — and what changes in 2027.

Venera Khassanovareal estate consultant · Greece and Bulgaria
Bulgaria & GreeceVENI Bulgaria or Greece: where is it better to buy property in 2027?
In short
  • Seaside space is cheaper in Bulgaria, and buying costs run about 3.5–5% versus 7–10% in Greece.
  • Rental income tax is effectively 9% in Bulgaria and starts at 15% in Greece.
  • Greece offers residency through property from €250,000, and a 15% transfer tax is planned for non-EU buyers from July 2027.

This is the question I hear most: “Venera, where should I buy — Bulgaria or Greece?” I work in both markets, so I will be honest: there is no one-size answer. But there are numbers that make the choice easier.

Price per square metre

Northern Greece is where prices are rising fastest. According to Spitogatos, the average asking price in Kavala in Q1 2026 was about €2,194 per m², up 13% in a year. In Burgas, Bulgarian agencies put it at about €1,700 per m². So the same money buys roughly a fifth more space in Burgas.

Bulgarian resorts vary a lot: a studio in an older complex can cost under €1,200 per m², while a new beachfront building is noticeably more expensive.

What the purchase itself costs

Bulgaria:

  • local acquisition tax — 0.1% to 3% (usually 2.5–3% in big cities and on the coast);
  • notary — set by tariff, capped at about €3,000 plus VAT;
  • registration — 0.1%.

In total, usually 3.5–5% on top of the price, including a lawyer and translator.

Greece:

  • property transfer tax — 3.09%;
  • notary — around 1% plus VAT;
  • lawyer — 1–1.5% plus VAT;
  • land registry/cadastre — around 0.5%.

Here, budget 7–10%.

What changes in 2027

A bill is now before the Greek parliament: for home buyers who are tax residents outside the EU, the transfer tax would rise from 3% to 15% on deals signed from 1 July 2027. It is not law yet (more on the new tax), but if you are from outside the EU and looking at Greece, it is better not to wait.

Rental tax and running costs

  • Bulgaria: rental income is taxed at 10% after a 10% standard deduction — effectively 9%. Annual property tax and the waste fee are usually low.
  • Greece: from 2026 the scale is 15% up to €12,000 a year, 25% up to €24,000, then 35% and 45%. Plus the annual ENFIA property tax.

Residency

Simply buying a flat in Bulgaria does not give you a residence permit. Greece has the Golden Visa — from €250,000, a 5-year permit for the whole family.

My conclusion

  • You need residency through property — Greece.
  • You want an affordable home by the sea and low taxes — Bulgaria.
  • Yield matters most — compare specific properties, not countries: the area and the building make a big difference.
