Buying property in Bulgaria as a foreigner: documents, taxes and hidden costs

Step by step: how a foreigner buys a flat or house in Bulgaria, which documents to check, what you pay the notary and the state, and the costs nobody mentions.

Venera Khassanovareal estate consultant · Greece and Bulgaria
BulgariaVENI Buying property in Bulgaria as a foreigner: documents, taxes and hidden costs
In short
  • Foreigners buy flats on the same terms as Bulgarians; non-EU citizens cannot buy land directly.
  • Budget 3.5–5% on top of the price: local tax, notary, registration, lawyer and translator.
  • Also allow time and money for translations, apostilles, a power of attorney and the bank's source-of-funds check.

Bulgaria is one of the easiest EU countries to buy a home in. It has used the euro since 2026, it is in Schengen, and the deal is completed at a notary in a single day. But “easy” does not mean “no pitfalls”. Here is how it works.

Who can buy what

  • A flat, a house without land, an apartment in a complex — a foreigner buys in their own name, just like a Bulgarian.
  • Land (a plot, a house with a garden) — EU citizens buy directly. Non-EU citizens usually buy through a Bulgarian company. It is a legal and common structure, but it has to be set up properly.

Step by step

  1. Choosing and checking. A lawyer reviews the seller's title deed, the cadastral sketch, the tax valuation certificate and any encumbrances (mortgage, seizure, disputes).
  2. Preliminary contract. Fixes the price, the timeline and a deposit — usually 10%.
  3. Signing at the notary. You sign the notarial deed. If you do not speak Bulgarian, a sworn translator is required.
  4. Payment. By law, amounts over €5,113 can only be paid by bank transfer, so the price is wired to the seller's account.
  5. Registration. The notary files the deed with the Property Register, and you become the owner.
  6. Municipal declaration. Within two months you file a declaration so that property tax and the waste fee are charged to you.

What it costs

  • Local acquisition tax — 0.1% to 3%, set by the municipality. In Sofia, Burgas and on the coast it is usually 2.5–3%.
  • Notary — by state tariff; the higher the price, the lower the percentage; capped at about €3,068 plus VAT.
  • Registration — 0.1% of the price.
  • Lawyer — usually €500–1,500.
  • Translator — if needed.

In total, around 3.5–5% on top of the price. For a €100,000 flat, the mandatory payments come to roughly €3,500–4,000.

Costs people often forget

  • Translations and legalisation. If documents (such as a power of attorney or a marital status certificate) were issued abroad, you need an apostille and a sworn translation.
  • Power of attorney. If you cannot come to the signing, sign a power of attorney at a notary in your country, with an apostille and translation. It is quicker than it sounds, but allow 1–2 weeks.
  • A company to buy land. If you are from outside the EU and buying a house with a plot, budget for registering a Bulgarian company and its annual accounting.
  • The bank. The bank will check where the money comes from — prepare statements or the sale contract of your previous home in advance, or the transfer will be delayed.

What to check in the flat and the complex itself (maintenance fee, Act 16, debts) is covered in detail in my checklist for choosing a flat.

After the purchase

Every year you pay property tax and the waste fee — usually small amounts. If you let the flat, income tax is effectively 9% of the rent.
