Burgas or Kavala: where is better to buy a home for year-round living?

Two of my favourite seaside towns — Burgas in Bulgaria and Kavala in Greece. I compare prices, infrastructure, healthcare, transport, climate and what winter life is like.

Venera Khassanovareal estate consultant · Greece and Bulgaria

In short
  • Burgas is a big city with an airport and year-round life, and homes are cheaper.
  • Kavala is smaller and cosier, with a mild climate and fast-rising prices: +13% in a year.
  • For year-round living, the neighbourhood, heating and distance to a doctor and an airport matter most.

I live between Bulgaria and Greece, and I know these two towns very well. Burgas and Kavala have a lot in common: both are on the sea, with a port, a promenade and a character of their own. But for living there all year round, the difference shows.

Size and scale

Burgas is Bulgaria's fourth-largest city, with about 200,000 people. It has everything: shopping centres, universities, theatres, large hospitals and sports clubs. Life does not stop in winter.

Kavala is a small town of about 70,000 people including its surroundings. An old town with a fortress, an aqueduct and a port with ferries to Thassos. The pace is calmer, everything is close by, and people know each other.

Property prices

  • Burgas: on average about €1,700 per m², more in new complexes and the centre.
  • Kavala: the average asking price is about €2,194 per m² according to Spitogatos, up 13% in a year — and 68% since 2022.

So the same money buys more space in Burgas, while Kavala is currently one of the fastest-growing markets in Northern Greece.

Rent

In Kavala, rent is around €6.5–7.3 per m² a month and rising: up 12% in a year according to RE/MAX. In Burgas, long-term rent is steady too — it is a student and working city, so flats do not stand empty.

Transport

  • Burgas: an international airport with flights across Europe, trains and buses to Sofia, and a port.
  • Kavala: its own airport with seasonal flights; year-round it is easier to fly via Thessaloniki, about two hours by car. Ferries to Thassos run daily.

Healthcare

Burgas has several large public and private hospitals and many private clinics. Kavala has a general hospital and private doctors, and many people go to Thessaloniki for complex treatment. If healthcare nearby matters to you, Burgas has the edge.

Climate and winter

Kavala is sheltered by mountains, so winter is milder and there are more sunny days. Burgas can be windy and cooler in winter, with the occasional snowfall. Summers are hot in both, but the sea keeps the air fresh.

In winter, heating matters: in Kavala it is often central heating on oil or air conditioning; in Burgas it is air conditioning, electric heating or district heating in some areas.

My conclusion

  • Burgas — if you want a big city, healthcare close by, direct flights and more affordable prices.
  • Kavala — if you want a quiet Greek lifestyle, a mild winter and an investment in a growing market. Plus, Greece offers the Golden Visa.
