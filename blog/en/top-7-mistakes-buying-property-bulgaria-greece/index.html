Top 7 mistakes when buying property in Bulgaria and Greece

A deposit without checks, an understated price in the contract, illegal extensions, the wrong owner and money the bank would not release — the seven mistakes I see buyers make most often.

Venera Khassanovareal estate consultant · Greece and Bulgaria
Bulgaria & GreeceVENI Top 7 mistakes when buying property in Bulgaria and Greece
In short
  • The costliest mistake is paying a deposit before the legal checks.
  • Do not understate the price in the contract or pay in cash: in both countries it backfires on the buyer.
  • Decide in advance whose name the property goes in, and prepare proof of where your money comes from.

Over the years I have seen people delighted with their keys — and disappointed a year later by things that could have been settled in advance. I have already written a detailed checklist on choosing the flat itself. This article is about something else: mistakes in the deal.

1. A deposit before the documents are checked

You want to “reserve” the property, so you send a deposit the same day. Then a mortgage, a seizure or a second owner turns up. First the lawyer's check, then the deposit — and only with a written contract that says when the deposit is refundable.

2. An understated price and cash

Sometimes a seller suggests writing a lower price in the contract “to save on tax”. That is a risk for you: if the deal falls through, the “unofficial” part is almost impossible to recover, and when you resell you pay more tax on the difference. In Greece the tax is charged on the “objective” value anyway if it is higher. In Bulgaria, amounts over €5,113 can by law only be paid by bank transfer.

3. Unauthorised extensions in Greece

An enclosed balcony, an “extra” room or an attic that is not on the plans is problem number one in Greece. Without an engineer's certificate the deal will not be signed, and legalisation costs time and money. Find out before paying a deposit — here is how the checks work.

4. Not thinking about whose name it goes in

  • In Bulgaria, non-EU citizens cannot buy land in their own name — a house with a plot is bought through a Bulgarian company.
  • In Greece, for the Golden Visa it matters who the owner is and how the purchase is structured, and such property cannot be let short-term.
  • Spouses, children, shares — decide in advance, because transferring later costs extra taxes and fees.

5. Not preparing the money

Banks in both countries check where the money comes from. Without statements, the sale contract of your previous home or proof of income, the transfer can be held up — while the contract deadlines keep running. For Greece you need an AFM tax number and a Greek bank account in advance.

6. Counting only the listing price

Add buying costs (about 3.5–5% in Bulgaria and 7–10% in Greece), renovation, furniture, annual taxes and upkeep. A good purchase is one that fits your budget with all of that included.

7. Buying “for holiday lets” without checking

Short-term letting requires registration: in Bulgaria under the Tourism Act, in Greece an AMA registry number. In some parts of Greece new registrations are frozen altogether. Check the address first and work out the net income — I went through this using a real deal.

How I work so these mistakes do not happen

  • I check the documents with a lawyer before any deposit;
  • in Greece I bring in an engineer;
  • we agree in advance whose name the property goes in and how the money will move;
  • I calculate the full cost of buying and owning.
