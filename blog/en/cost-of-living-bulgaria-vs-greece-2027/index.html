The cost of living in Bulgaria and Greece in 2027: a detailed comparison

Rent, groceries, utilities, transport and healthcare: I compare how much a single person and a family need per month in Bulgaria and in Greece.

Venera Khassanovareal estate consultant · Greece and Bulgaria
Bulgaria & GreeceVENI The cost of living in Bulgaria and Greece in 2027: a detailed comparison
In short
  • Bulgaria is cheaper than Greece across the board, but the biggest gap is housing.
  • A basic budget for one person in Bulgaria is about €710 a month, before entertainment.
  • For the same life in Greece, budget roughly 15–30% more — and rent is noticeably higher.

Many of my clients choose a country not only by the price of a flat but by what everyday life will cost. Let us look at the average figures — and then I will tell you what they look like by the sea.

The big picture

Different services calculate differently, but the conclusion is the same: Bulgaria is cheaper. According to Livingcost, life for one person in Bulgaria costs about 16% less than in Greece, and for a family about 13% less. Other services show a bigger gap — up to 30–50%, especially compared with Athens.

Rent

This is where the difference is largest. Average rent for a one-bedroom flat is about €400 in Bulgaria and about €480 in Greece (WhereToEmigrate). In resort towns it depends on the season: summer is pricier, winter is negotiable.

In Kavala, according to Spitogatos and RE/MAX, rent is around €6.5–7.3 per m² a month, so a 60 m² flat costs roughly €400–440.

Groceries

  • Bulgaria: about €200 a month per person;
  • Greece: about €255.

Vegetables, fruit, bread and dairy are inexpensive in both countries, especially at markets. Imported goods and supermarket brands cost more.

Utilities and transport

Electricity, water, internet and mobile are usually cheaper in Bulgaria. A public transport pass is about €28–30 in both countries. In winter, how the flat is heated matters a lot: air conditioning, electric heaters or heating oil — the bill can differ several times over.

Healthcare

Both countries have a public system, but foreigners more often use private clinics and insurance. A private doctor's visit and lab tests are generally cheaper in Bulgaria. Health insurance is required for residence permits in both countries.

Budgets: single and family

One person, modest (rent, food, transport, utilities):

  • Bulgaria — about €710 a month;
  • Greece — roughly 15–30% more.

A family (Livingcost, all expenses included):

  • Bulgaria — about $2,590 a month;
  • Greece — about $2,990.

If you own your home, the budget gets much lighter straight away: only utilities, property tax and, in a complex, the maintenance fee remain.

My conclusion

  • You want to live as economically as possible — Bulgaria, especially seaside towns outside the resorts: Burgas, Pomorie.
  • Climate, food and residency through property matter more — Greece, but plan a bigger budget.
