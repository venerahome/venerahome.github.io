What can you buy for €50,000, €80,000 and €100,000 in Bulgaria and Greece?

Not theory but real properties from my catalogue: what you can buy by the sea in Bulgaria and Greece today on three popular budgets — with the pros and cons.

Venera Khassanovareal estate consultant · Greece and Bulgaria
Bulgaria & GreeceVENI What can you buy for €50,000, €80,000 and €100,000 in Bulgaria and Greece?
In short
  • €50,000 buys a seaside studio in either country or a small house in a Bulgarian village.
  • €80,000 buys a flat with a separate bedroom, or a 60–80 m² flat in Kavala.
  • €100,000 buys a seaside one-bedroom in Bulgaria or a house near the beach in Kavala to renovate.

The most common question in my messages is: “I have this much — what can I buy?” So I have gathered real properties from my catalogue (prices as of November 2026) and sorted them into three budgets. Do not forget to add buying costs: about 3.5–5% in Bulgaria and 7–10% in Greece.

Up to €50,000

Bulgaria:

  • a 31 m² studio with a pool view, Sunny Beach — €52,000 (almost within budget, with room to negotiate);
  • a new 44 m² timber-frame house with a 500 m² plot, Kableshkovo, 15 minutes from Burgas — €42,800.

Greece (Kavala):

  • a 30 m² studio with its own outdoor space, Dexameni — €40,000;
  • a new 26 m² studio with a high-end renovation and furniture, Timios Stavros — €47,000.

Pros: a low entry point, easy to let, minimal running costs.

Cons: small size; in resort complexes, a maintenance fee that lowers the yield.

Around €80,000

Bulgaria:

  • a 41 m² flat with a separate bedroom, Sunny Beach — €78,000;
  • furnished 44 m² apartments in Nessebar, Kokalu — €65,000.

Greece (Kavala):

  • a furnished 60 m² flat with its own entrance in the centre — €80,000;
  • an 80 m² two-bedroom flat in Potamoudia — €75,000;
  • a 74 m² three-bedroom flat with a view in Agios Georgios — €85,000.

What you see: in Kavala this money buys a city flat to live in; in Bulgaria, a resort flat for holidays and letting. Greece gives you more space, but buildings are usually older, so set money aside for renovation.

Around €100,000

Bulgaria:

  • a 71 m² one-bedroom with a sea view 50 m from the shore, Ravda — €101,000;
  • a new build in Burgas, a 60 m² one-bedroom with no agency fee — €100,000;
  • a new 112 m² house, Kableshkovo — €93,000.

Greece (Kavala):

  • a detached 91 m² house with a yard 150 m from the beach, Rapsani (needs renovation) — €91,000;
  • a renovated, furnished 57 m² flat, Dexameni — €97,000;
  • a new furnished 31 m² studio, Vyronas — €87,000.

What you see: €100,000 is already a choice between “ready and by the sea” in Bulgaria and “your own house near the beach to renovate” in Greece.

How to choose

  • To live in — a city flat: Burgas or Kavala.
  • For summer letting — a resort studio, but factor in the fee and taxes.
  • For long-term letting — a small city flat near a university or the centre.
