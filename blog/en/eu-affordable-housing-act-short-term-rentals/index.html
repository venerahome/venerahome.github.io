The EU will let cities restrict Airbnb. What it means for buyers in Greece and Bulgaria

The European Commission has proposed an Affordable Housing Act, and in October the European Parliament debated it for the first time. Here is what it says, what already applies in Greece and Bulgaria, and how to choose a rental property now.

In short
  • The EU is not banning short-term rentals, but it gives cities clear rules for restricting them in hot spots — for up to 5 years.
  • New Airbnb listings can no longer be registered in central Athens and Thessaloniki — until the end of 2027, the government says.
  • When choosing a rental property, check the area and run the numbers for long-term letting too.

If you are buying a flat by the sea to rent to tourists on Airbnb or Booking, this news affects you directly. On 9 September the European Commission presented a draft Affordable Housing Act, and in October the European Parliament debated it for the first time. Here is what it says, what already applies in Greece and Bulgaria, and how to choose a rental property now.

What Brussels is proposing

Housing in Europe is getting more expensive faster than wages are rising, and in tourist centres locals are finding it harder and harder to rent. The new act does not ban short-term rentals and does not take housing policy away from member states. It gives cities and regions clear rules on when and how they can restrict them without breaking EU single-market rules.

The key points of the draft:

  • Restrictions are only possible in "housing stress areas" — clearly defined areas where homes have become too expensive for residents. Under the draft, prices there must be 8 or more times annual income, and that ratio must be rising.
  • Measures must be proportionate, non-discriminatory and temporary — up to 5 years, with a possible extension after review.
  • If you occasionally rent out your own main home, the restrictions do not apply to you.
  • New purchase conditions can only apply to homes bought after the act takes effect. Local rules adopted earlier stay in place.
  • Foreigners cannot be singled out: the rules are the same for everyone, regardless of nationality.

When it will apply

For now it is a draft. On 6 October it was debated for the first time at a plenary session of the European Parliament in Strasbourg, and the EU Council has started examining it. MEPs have no common position yet: some want tougher measures, others a more cautious approach. Negotiations will take months. Once adopted, the regulation will enter into force 20 days after publication and apply directly in every EU country.

What already applies in Greece

Greece did not wait for Brussels. In central Athens — municipal districts 1, 2 and 3 (Plaka, Kolonaki, Koukaki, Syntagma, Monastiraki, Exarchia and others) — new properties cannot be registered for short-term rental. Since 1 July the same freeze has applied in Thessaloniki's historic centre. In September the government announced that the freezes would be extended until 31 December 2027.

An important detail: when a flat with an active registration number (AMA) is sold, the number is cancelled, and the new owner cannot get a new one while the freeze lasts. So a seller's line that "the flat is already on Airbnb" guarantees nothing in these areas.

Illegal short-term letting is costly: the AADE tax authority fines 50% of the rental income, with a minimum of €20,000.

Demand is still strong, though: according to AirDNA's July data, the average nightly rate in Greece rose 12.8% year on year, to €200.35.

And in Bulgaria?

Bulgaria has no short-term rental freeze zones so far. But here too, as across the EU, a common European regulation on short-term rental registration has applied since 20 May 2026: each property needs a registration number, and platforms share data with the authorities. Off-the-books letting is becoming a thing of the past everywhere.

What this means for buyers

I don't think renting out a home by the sea has become a bad idea. But the rules are changing, so the numbers need more care. My advice:

  1. Look at the area, not just the flat. If it is already very expensive and tourists clearly outnumber residents, restrictions may appear within a few years.
  2. Run the yield for long-term letting too. If the numbers work with a one-year tenant, the purchase is protected from any new rules.
  3. In Greece, ask about the AMA before paying a deposit. Check which zone the flat is in and whether a new owner can get a registration.
  4. Remember the Golden Visa rule. A home bought under the programme cannot be let short term — that is a rule of the programme itself.
