Bulgaria's new Foreigners Act: what changes for permanent residence, work and documents

On 18 November parliament finally adopted the amendments to the Foreigners Act. Here is what they mean for people with long-term or permanent residence, for those working in Bulgaria and for anyone planning to move.

In short
  • A permanent resident may lose that status after spending less than 6 months and 1 day in Bulgaria in the previous year; investors are exempt.
  • Foreigners with long-term residence will be able to change employer and will pay health insurance contributions.
  • The law still has to be signed by the President and published in the State Gazette before it takes effect.

On 18 November the National Assembly adopted, at second and final reading, the amendments to the Foreigners in the Republic of Bulgaria Act. Many of my clients live here on long-term or permanent residence, so I looked closely at what is changing. One important caveat first: the amendments are not in force yet.

The key point for permanent residents

According to the text debated in the chamber, a permanent resident may lose that status if they spent less than 6 months and 1 day in Bulgaria in the previous calendar year.

Investors who obtained permanent residence on the investment grounds in the law are exempt. In the chamber, MPs proposed extending the exemption to their family members and taking valid reasons for absence into account — treatment abroad or caring for a sick relative.

What this means in practice: if you hold permanent residence but spend more of your time in another country, count your days in advance. Keep tickets, rental contracts and bills — anything that proves you were in Bulgaria.

Work: a single permit and protection for workers

  • A single residence and work permit is introduced. The procedure takes up to 2 months, extendable by 30 days in complex cases.
  • Foreigners with long-term residence will be able to change employer.
  • If a foreigner loses the right to work through the employer's fault, the employer must pay compensation.
  • Unemployment does not automatically cost you the permit: up to 3 months is allowed, or up to 6 months if the single permit has been held for more than two years.
  • Employers who provide housing for seasonal workers must sign a written contract with them setting out the terms.

Health insurance

Foreigners with long-term residence will pay health insurance contributions in Bulgaria and in return get access to public healthcare. For many people this is good news: until now they often had to rely on private insurance alone.

Document deadlines

The ordinary service for issuing identity documents is cut from 30 to 20 days. The fast service for replacing a temporary identity certificate takes up to 3 working days.

When it takes effect

The President has up to 15 days to sign the decree promulgating the law or return it to parliament. After publication in the State Gazette, a law usually enters into force 3 days later, unless other dates are set for specific provisions. The final text will soon appear on the State Gazette website, where the exact dates can be checked.

My advice

  • You hold permanent residence and travel a lot — count your days for 2026 and plan 2027 so that you spend more than half the year in Bulgaria.
  • You are only planning to move — choose the route that fits your lifestyle. Details on each: for retirees, through investment, work, study, family and business.
  • You are employed — the new rules protect you better, but keep copies of your contracts and documents.
