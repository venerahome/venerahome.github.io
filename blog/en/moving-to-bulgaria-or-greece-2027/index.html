Moving to Bulgaria or Greece: residency, documents, housing and costs in 2027

Which residence routes Bulgaria and Greece offer, how much income you need, what insurance to get, where to live in the first months and what owning property gives you.

Venera Khassanovareal estate consultant · Greece and Bulgaria
Bulgaria & GreeceVENI Moving to Bulgaria or Greece: residency, documents, housing and costs in 2027
In short
  • Bulgaria grants retirees residency with a pension of about €620 a month; Greece requires €3,500 for financially independent persons.
  • Buying a flat in Bulgaria does not give residency; in Greece, the Golden Visa through property starts at €250,000.
  • Every route requires health insurance, an address and proof of income.

I once moved to Bulgaria myself with three small children and started from scratch, so I know how many questions come up at the beginning. Here is the essentials in one place.

If you are an EU citizen

It is simple: you can live and work in both countries; you just register your address and get a residence document. The rest of this article is for people coming from outside the EU.

Residence routes in Bulgaria

  • Retirees — a pension of at least the minimum wage, about €620 a month in 2026. First a D visa, then a one-year permit renewed annually. Details
  • Digital nomads — annual income of 50 minimum wages, about €27,500 for applications in 2026. Details
  • Investment — from €306,775 under the rules set by law. Simply buying a flat does not give residency. Details
  • Work, study, family, your own company — the classic routes.

Residence routes in Greece

  • Financially independent persons — passive income from €3,500 a month (plus 20% for a spouse and 15% per child). Working in Greece is not allowed. Details
  • Digital nomads — income from €3,500 a month working for foreign employers. Details
  • Golden Visa — property from €250,000, a 5-year permit for the whole family, with no requirement to live there full time.
  • Work, study, family. All options

Documents you almost always need

  • passport and a criminal record certificate;
  • proof of income (bank statements, pension certificate);
  • health insurance valid in the country;
  • an address — a rental contract or a title deed;
  • translations and apostilles — allow time and money for them.

Housing in the first months

My advice: do not buy on your first visit. Rent for a few months, live through a winter and explore the neighbourhoods. A one-bedroom flat rents for about €400 in Bulgaria and about €480 in Greece on average.

What owning property gives you

  • in Bulgaria — an address for your permit, stability and savings on rent, but not the permit itself;
  • in Greece — the same, and from €250,000 the Golden Visa too;
  • in both — the option to let the home while you are not living in it.

If you are from outside the EU and choosing Greece, note that from 1 July 2027 a 15% transfer tax instead of 3% is planned for home buyers who are tax residents outside the EU. The bill is already in parliament — more about it.

Moving costs

Work them out in advance: visas and fees, translations, insurance, the first months of rent plus a deposit, furniture and appliances, and shipping your belongings. Life in Bulgaria is cheaper on average than in Greece — worth building into your budget.

My conclusion

  • You want an affordable life by the sea and an easy route for retirees — Bulgaria.
  • You want residency through property for the whole family — Greece.
