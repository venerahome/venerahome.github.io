A seaside flat to let: Bulgaria or Greece — where is the yield higher?

I run the real numbers: what long-term and short-term letting bring in Burgas and Kavala, which taxes you pay and what is left in your pocket.

Venera Khassanovareal estate consultant · Greece and Bulgaria
Bulgaria & GreeceVENI A seaside flat to let: Bulgaria or Greece — where is the yield higher?
In short
  • Long-term letting in Burgas yields about 4.6% a year before tax, in Kavala about 3.8–4%.
  • After rental income tax (9% in Bulgaria, from 15% in Greece), Bulgaria usually comes out ahead.
  • Short-term letting by the sea looks more lucrative, but net yield after costs is often just 3–3.5%.

“I want to buy a flat by the sea and let it out” — that is how half of my client conversations begin. Let us do the maths honestly, without advertising percentages.

Long-term letting: what the numbers say

Bulgaria, Burgas. According to Global Property Guide, the gross rental yield in Burgas is about 4.6% a year, one of the highest among the country's big cities. The city lives all year round: students, professionals, families.

Greece, Kavala. The average asking price is about €2,194 per m², and rent is about €7 per m² a month (Spitogatos, RE/MAX). That gives a gross yield of roughly 3.8–4%. On the other hand, rents in Kavala are rising fast — up 12% in a year.

Rental income tax

  • Bulgaria: 10% on income after an automatic 10% deduction — effectively 9% of the rent.
  • Greece: from 2026 — 15% on income up to €12,000 a year, 25% up to €24,000, then 35% and 45%.

An example for €100,000

Burgas: rent of about €4,600 a year, tax of about €414 — roughly €4,190 left, or about 4.2%.

Kavala: rent of about €3,800–4,000 a year, tax of about €570–600 — roughly €3,230–3,400 left, about 3.3%.

From these amounts, also deduct property tax, insurance, small repairs and empty periods between tenants. With long-term letting, a flat pays for itself in roughly 20–25 years — plus the growth in the property's value.

Short-term letting

Nightly rates look good in summer, but after the maintenance fee, management, taxes and an empty winter, the net yield by the sea is often around 3–3.5%. I covered this in detail for Bulgaria in the article “coast or city” and for Greece using a real deal in Athens. Just the essentials here: short-term letting requires registration in both countries, and Golden Visa property in Greece cannot be let short-term.

Where is the yield higher?

  • Net yield from long-term letting — usually higher in Bulgaria: a lower price per square metre and lower tax.
  • Capital growth — currently stronger in Northern Greece: Kavala +13% in a year.
  • A combination — in a seaside city (Burgas, Pomorie, Kavala) you can let long-term in winter and nightly in summer.

My advice

Do not compare countries — compare specific flats: area, floor, fee, heating, demand. Send me your budget and I will calculate the net yield for several real properties in both countries.
